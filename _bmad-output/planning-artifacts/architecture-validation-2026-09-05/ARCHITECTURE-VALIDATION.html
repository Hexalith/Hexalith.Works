Critical Five decision blockers

VAL-C01 — Platform-host ownership and migration allocation are unbound

Issue: Works-owned AppHost, ServiceDefaults, delivery, scheduling, projections/queries, and subscriptions are prohibited, but the destination repository, accountable owner, and seam-by-seam capability allocation are unnamed.

Divergence/risk: Works can delete adapter behavior while the platform composes only the minimal executable; reminders, cascades, checkpoints, subscriptions, and projections then have no owner. Story 4.9 is blocked.

Source: architecture.md:151–179, 506–508, 524–528, 586–591, 631–647.

Live/spec evidence: the mandatory boundary forbids domain-owned plumbing (hexalith-llm-instructions.md:121–134); the solution retains both host projects (Hexalith.Works.slnx:50–58); Works registers its runtime behaviors (WorksHost.cs:64–121); AppHost owns Dapr/topic/store composition (Program.cs:54–136); the approved proposal requires target and owner (sprint-change-proposal:377–406, 427–431). The graph mixes Aspire AppHost SDK 13.4.6 with hosting 13.5.3; the cited official line is Aspire.Hosting 13.5.3.

Lenses: RW-01, ADV-03, TECH-02. Disposition: Discuss. Name repository, team, packages, supported Aspire family, conformance lane, and migration matrix before Story 4.9 or deletion. No autofix was applied.

VAL-C02 — Tree mutation has no authoritative atomic owner

Issue: A pure aggregate promises a single-parent, acyclic, bounded, tenant-closed tree without assigning who reserves the child edge, derives ancestry/depth, or serializes competing attaches.

Divergence/risk: Two compliant parents can accept the same child from stale or empty caller facts, creating multiple parents or a cycle.

Source: architecture.md:34–36, 96, 244, 276, 285.

Live evidence: SpawnChild.cs:10–32 accepts caller-fed facts; WorkTreeAttachmentGuard.cs:30–69 can only inspect those facts; work-tree-shape-guard.md:35–42 confirms no authoritative store read.

Lenses: ADV-01, SEC-DI-03. Disposition: Discuss. Choose child-owned conditional attachment, tree registry, or saga; bind authority, freshness/version, concurrency, conflict, and repair. No autofix was applied.

VAL-C03 — Incremental recursive roll-up cannot run through the stated seam

Issue: The architecture binds incremental descendant-to-ancestor roll-up, but live EventStore /project supplies one aggregate stream and cannot reconcile later child changes.

Divergence/risk: Works has no graph input while the platform legitimately persists roll-up as unavailable; FR-11 is delivered only after an operator rebuild.

Source: architecture.md:36, 96, 239, 276, 623–629.

Live/spec evidence: prd.md:191–198 requires incremental ancestor updates; WorkItemProjectionDispatcher.cs:18–37 documents the aggregate-local limitation; work-roll-up-projection.md:67–100 says only shared rebuild yields a coherent tree.

Lens: ADV-02. Disposition: Discuss. Choose durable relationship fan-out, explicit child contributions, or a rebuild-only product contract and bind ownership, inputs, ordering, failure, freshness, and unavailable semantics. No autofix was applied.

VAL-C04 — Tenant keys are not authorization or identity provenance

Issue: Tenant scoping and query authorization are required, but no authoritative tenant/actor source, principal binding, delegation model, or action policy is defined; “real auth” is deferred.

Divergence/risk: A caller can select another tenant and pass every equality check; key prefixes only prevent accidental collisions after identity is trusted.

Source: architecture.md:44, 244–246, 402–404.

Live evidence: commands carry caller tenant values (CreateWorkItem.cs:18–29); queries use envelope tenant directly (WhatsNextQueryHandler.cs:47–65, GetWorkItemQueryHandler.cs:47–61); missing predicates authorize (WhatsNextQueryAuthorization.cs:6–33).

Lens: SEC-DI-01. Disposition: Discuss. Bind authentication, derived claims, mismatch denial, workload delegation, policy ownership, and command/query/admin/rebuild/replay authorization. No autofix was applied.

VAL-C05 — Events, reminders, replay, and callbacks lack trusted-origin rules

Issue: Delivery rules cover duplicates and order, not producer authenticity or direct-route protection.

Divergence/risk: A forged self-consistent high-sequence event can poison watermarks and trigger cascade, expiry, cancellation, or resume. Idempotency does not authenticate origin.

Source: architecture.md:251–254, 631–647.

Live evidence: the route has no app authentication (WorksDomainEventEndpointExtensions.cs:16–28); config says production must add mTLS (accesscontrol.works.yaml:8–9); processing validates only supplied-field agreement (WorksDomainEventProcessor.cs:117–145) before side effects (WorksHost.cs:68–82).

Lens: SEC-DI-02. Disposition: Discuss. Bind workload/sidecar identity, trust domain, deny-by-default reachability, TLS/ACLs, exclusive producers, and negative migration tests. No autofix was applied.

High Twelve convergent-contract defects

VAL-H01 — Automatic expiry has no trigger, policy authority, or recovery design

Issue: C2 schedules resume only; E2 leaves Due-Date/TTL source unresolved. No owner schedules, cancels, reschedules, emits, or recovers ExpireWorkItem. Risk: compliant implementations can deliver no expiry, Due-Date-only, TTL-only, or incompatible recovery.

Source: architecture.md:261–267, 649–655. Evidence: prd.md:175–183; ExpireWorkItem.cs:6–10; WorksHost.cs:64–89.

Lens: RW-02. Disposition: Discuss. Bind policy authority, trigger owner, registration identity, reschedule/cancel, delivery/idempotency, and missed-firing recovery—or change FR-10. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H02 — The dependency rule contradicts the machine-enforced graph

Issue/risk: Contracts ← Server ← Projections says Projections depends on Server; live rules make Server, Projections, and Reactor direct Contracts dependants. Following the source creates a forbidden reference.

Source: architecture.md:326–334, 530–532. Evidence: Projections.csproj:8–10; DependencyDirectionTests.cs:7–25; boundary-decision-record.md:138–140.

Lens: RW-03. Disposition: Autofix in Update. State Server → Contracts, Projections → Contracts, Reactor → Contracts. Validation applied no change.

VAL-H03 — “Creation order” is absent and replaced by ID order

Issue/risk: A2/FR-20 require Priority → Due Date → creation order, but no comparable creation coordinate exists and the live comparer uses arbitrary WorkItem ID. Different producers/consumers can return opposite orders.

Source: architecture.md:232–240, 614–621. Evidence: prd.md:281–287; epics.md:1141–1157; WhatsNextOrdering.cs:62–71; WhatsNextItem.cs:27–38; whats-next-projection.md:16–32.

Lenses: RW-04, ADV-08. Disposition: Discuss. Add a replay-stable creation coordinate and tie rules, or approve identity order across PRD/epics/architecture. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H04 — The legacy shape has unstable decision identity

Issue/risk: Analysis, rationale, seed, repeated rules, and self-validation coexist without stable AD-n or Binds/Prevents/Rule fields; D-1/D1 and D-2/D2 collide. References can target the wrong decision and duplicated prose drifts.

Source: architecture.md:22–24, 90–129, 209–287, 444–591, 593–732. Evidence: lint-spine.json and RW-05.

Lens: RW-05. Disposition: Discuss. Distill stable decision blocks, retain a legacy-label map, and subordinate volatile seed to authoritative code. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H05 — Version claims are stale and conflate Dapr runtime with SDK

Issue/risk: The source mixes .NET 10.0.300/10.0.301, Dapr 1.18.4, xUnit 3.2.2, Fluent rc.3, and unsupported “current/compatible” wording. Live pins are .NET 10.0.400, Dapr .NET SDK 1.18.5, xUnit 4.0.0, Fluent rc.5, with Aspire 13.4.6/13.5.3 mixed. Builders can choose the wrong runtime or downgrade packages.

Source: architecture.md:61, 189–202, 263, 453–455, 595–601. Evidence: global.json:1–11; central pins:109–146, 226–227, 318–321.

Official evidence: .NET SDK 10.0.400, Dapr .NET SDK releases, xUnit 4.0.0/MTP v2, Fluent UI history, Aspire.Hosting 13.5.3, Dapr runtime releases.

Lenses: RW-06, TECH-01/02/03. Disposition: Autofix in Update for authoritative pin sources and separate runtime/SDK fields; Discuss Aspire family and production runtime pin. Validation applied no change.

VAL-H06 — Cascade checkpoints are neither concurrency-safe nor operationally fail-safe

Issue/risk: No checkpoint key/state machine, monotonic concurrency, message identity, replica ownership, durable retry, health degradation, or alerting is bound. Concurrent reconcilers can regress completion or strand indexes; exhausted recovery can remain healthy.

Source: architecture.md:99–101, 253, 374–377, 623–629. Evidence: CascadeDispatcher.cs:170–209; checkpoint store:7–13, 39–80; recovery service:19–32; reconciler:43–96.

Lenses: ADV-04, SEC-DI-05. Disposition: Discuss. Bind monotonic CAS/fencing, deterministic identities, replica rules, index convergence, durable retry, degraded readiness, metrics/alerts, and audited disposition. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H07 — Reminder recovery and end-to-end durability are underspecified

Issue/risk: C2 calls reminders durable while discovery remains a gap. Registration can be lost, tenant-wide scan rejected, or one-shot callback exhausted without continuous recovery or unhealthy evidence.

Source: architecture.md:103, 261–264, 649–655, 706–710. Evidence: epics.md:1290–1299; IndexedPendingDateAwaitSource.cs:11–79; ReminderReconciliationService.cs:9–60; WorksRecoveryOptions.cs:19–29.

Official Dapr sources verify persisted registration while narrowing the end-to-end claim: Scheduler 1.15 and actor reminders.

Lenses: ADV-05, TECH-03, SEC-DI-05. Disposition: Discuss. Bind authoritative truth, discovery/index order, scan bounds/failure, Scheduler HA/backup, callback policy, continuous retry/alerting, cleanup, and the exact guarantee. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H08 — Shared rebuild has atomic visibility but no delivery fence

Issue/risk: Generation staging and marker-gated promotion exist, but no fence token, writer epoch, capture watermark, or delivery coordinator does. Live writes can be omitted or overwritten between inventory capture and Commit.

Source: architecture.md:47, 105–106, 265, 623–629. Evidence: work-roll-up-projection.md:75–84; whats-next-projection.md:73–82; EventStore route:374–400; atomic visibility:281–327; dispatcher:20–99, 346–403.

Lenses: ADV-06, TECH-04. Disposition: Discuss. Define admitted writers, epoch/token, capture watermark, rejection/queueing, atomic scope, abort/resume, and catch-up proof. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H09 — Universal key derivation conflicts with namespaces and privileged registries

Issue/risk: The source derives every key/topic/reminder/group from one tenant key, but live code uses a shared topic, hashed reminder, generation keys, notifier groups, and global recovery indexes. Subscribers can disagree, reminders can collide if tenant is omitted, and cross-tenant registries lack a governed exception.

Source: architecture.md:321–324, 402–404, 539–540. Evidence: Program.cs:57–69; DateReminderName.cs:30–53; WorksReadModelKeys.cs:24–59; whats-next-projection.md:137–145; tenant registry:3–11; checkpoint index:20–23.

Lenses: ADV-07, SEC-DI-06. Disposition: Discuss. Create a namespace/ownership table and choose tenant partitioning or a narrow isolated, ACL-protected, audited control-plane exception. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H10 — Aggregate ID assignment is not command idempotency

Issue/risk: A1 claims retries are idempotent from aggregate ID alone, without transport key reuse/scope, digest, retention, result replay, or deterministic internal-message identity. Clients can receive rejection versus deduplicated success for the same logical retry.

Source: architecture.md:234–235, 253, 374–377. Evidence: CreateWorkItem.cs:17–29; WorkItemAggregate.cs:18–25; SubmitCommandRequest.cs:5–26.

Lens: ADV-09. Disposition: Discuss. Bind tenant-scoped transport identity, retry reuse, digest collision, retention, exact-result replay, and deterministic reactor/reminder IDs; distinguish dedup, semantic no-op, and projection offsets. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H11 — Schema evolution and quarantine are not a compatibility contract

Issue/risk: “Additive/tolerant” does not bind wire names, defaults/nulls, compatibility direction, discriminator or enum behavior, rollout, or consistent malformed/unknown handling. Old readers can fail; subscriptions can acknowledge data that projections reject; rebuilds can silently omit evidence.

Source: architecture.md:63–65, 309–315, 359–361, 409–426. Evidence: Channel.cs:5–18; WorkItemV1Catalog.cs:9–18; EventShapeGovernanceTests.cs:18–44; WorksEventDecoder.cs:24–44; endpoint:69–81; dispatcher:108–117.

Lenses: ADV-10, SEC-DI-07. Disposition: Discuss. Bind a reader/writer matrix, wire/discriminator rules, expand-migrate-contract rollout, catalog authority, and durable fail-safe quarantine/replay. No autofix was applied.

VAL-H12 — Immutable event data has no privacy lifecycle

Issue/risk: “Do not log payloads” does not classify durable fields or bind minimization, encryption/key ownership, access audit, retention, legal hold, offboarding, erasure/crypto-shredding, DLQ, backup, or snapshot controls. Raw free text and identities can remain indefinitely unmanaged.

Source: architecture.md:49, 226–230, 406–407. Evidence: WorkItemCreated.cs:8–18; ProgressReported.cs:8–15; WorkItemAssigned.cs:8–13; WorkItemCreated.v1.json:1; ProgressReported.v1.json:1.

Lens: SEC-DI-04. Disposition: Discuss. Bind classification/minimization, references, encryption/key lifecycle, least privilege, audit, retention/hold/offboarding, and supported erasure before production admission. No autofix was applied.
