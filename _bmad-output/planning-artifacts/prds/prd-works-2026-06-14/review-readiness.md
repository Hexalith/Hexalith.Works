# Implementation Readiness Review — Hexalith.Works

## Verdict — blocked for coordinated implementation

The September PRD is strong enough to guide architecture, but the full handoff is not implementation-ready without guessing. Isolated pure-domain work can continue where the PRD is internally consistent; Epic 3's Registry/Reactor work, distributed integration tests, platform migration, and production/security acceptance are blocked by contradictory product rules, unresolved external contracts, and downstream artifacts that still encode the pre-amendment design.

The minimum safe gate is: resolve the Resume and parent-reference contradictions in the PRD, bind VAL-H10 before drafting the Registry story, close or turn the R4/R6/R7/R11 platform obligations into executable story acceptance, rebaseline architecture/epics to all 26 FRs, and attach the §9 trusted-origin controls to an owned production-admission story.

## Findings

### Critical

- **[critical] Resume has contradictory normative outcomes** (PRD §4.2 FR-6 legal-transition table; PRD §4.4 FR-15; architecture AD-13; epics Story 3.5) — FR-6 calls its table normative and says `InProgress × Resume = R`; FR-15 says that after a successful Resume returns the item to `InProgress`, replaying the consumed Await-Condition is `NoOp`. Architecture AD-13 still says every “no current match” is a no-op, while Story 3.5 follows the newer consumed-key-only rule. Architecture, implementation, and transition-matrix tests can therefore each produce a different valid-looking answer. *Fix:* Make FR-15 the single product rule, encode the conditional duplicate in FR-6's table, amend AD-13 and every lifecycle matrix, and add three matrix cases: current match accepted, consumed match after resume no-op, every other non-match rejected.
- **[critical] Registry-first child creation is bypassable and its idempotency prerequisite is unbound** (PRD §4.1 FR-1; §4.3 FR-13; §4.4 FR-16; §10; architecture AD-21 and VAL-H10; epics Requirements Inventory FR-13/FR-16 and Stories 3.1–3.2) — FR-1 still lets an ordinary builder/Executor Create with a parent reference, but FR-16 and AD-21 make registry reserve the only public attachment entry and restrict `SpawnChild` to the Reactor. Meanwhile VAL-H10 explicitly must bind before the Registry story because reserve→spawn→attach/release recovery depends on deterministic transport identity. Current stories instead validate tree shape “at spawn” and expose `SpawnChild` to the coordinator. *Fix:* Forbid a parent reference on ordinary Create (or define a distinct origin-restricted child-create contract carrying registry evidence); then bind R11 MessageId/IdempotencyKey derivation, retention, duplicate-result replay, payload-mismatch behavior, and causation chaining; then replace Stories 3.1–3.2 with Registry contract/state, reservation conflict, Reactor translation, attach/release/timeout, child creation, and recovery slices in that order.

### High

- **[high] Downstream plans are not baselined to the current 26-FR contract** (PRD §0 and §4.4 FR-26; architecture “Requirements Overview” and “Implementation Handoff”; epics Requirements Inventory, FR Coverage Map, and Epic 3; addendum “Downstream handoff”) — Architecture and epics still declare 25 FRs; FR-26 has no coverage; architecture's overview says one aggregate owns parent/children; and Epic 3 retains the caller-facing spawn and direct-child roll-up design. The addendum itself lists H1–H10 edits still owed. *Fix:* Run a formal correct-course pass/sprint-planning pass that updates the inventory, feature map, architecture narrative, every affected story, and the coverage table; require every FR and §9 NFR to map to an owning story plus an acceptance lane before implementation resumes.
- **[high] The mandatory identity/trusted-origin baseline has no executable delivery story** (PRD §4.2 FR-7, §4.4 FR-16/FR-26, §4.5 FR-19, §9 “Identity provenance & trusted origin”; architecture AD-23/AD-24; epics Story 4.9; addendum event-catalog sketch) — PRD §9 requires verified OIDC-derived actor/Tenant, membership mapping, deny-before-dispatch, workload identity plus tenant delegation, origin-restricted internal commands, mTLS/trust-domain controls, and production ingress prohibition. Story 4.9 carries none of those acceptance cases. The addendum also says acting identity comes from “the binding + EventStore envelope,” contradicting FR-7's rule that actor identity is never inferred from Executor Binding. *Fix:* Add an owned platform-security story (or explicit Story 4.9 slice) with positive and negative tests for claims derivation, membership, mismatch/non-disclosure, query filtering, workload delegation, forged sequence, direct `SpawnChild`, topic producer ACL, reminder registration under deny-by-default policy, and mTLS/trust domain; correct the addendum actor sentence; keep production admission closed until it passes.
- **[high] Eventual Registry/Reactor behavior has no measurable completion or failure contract** (PRD §4.4 FR-16/FR-26; §6.1; §11 SM-1; architecture VAL-H06/VAL-H07 and R6/R7; epics Stories 4.6–4.8) — The PRD accepts a “bounded” reservation timeout and an eventual window in which descendants can progress or complete after parent termination, but gives no bound, acknowledgement predicate, terminal checkpoint state, alert condition, or retry-exhaustion behavior. Stories require eventual convergence yet cannot determine how long to wait or when failure is proven. *Fix:* Define configured acceptance bounds and observable quiescence for reserve settlement, child creation, child-completion Resume, cascade, and reminder recovery; specify checkpoint monotonic states/CAS ownership, continuous-retry versus dead-letter policy, timeout disposition, and alert evidence; make SM-1 await acknowledgement rather than sleep.
- **[high] Roll-Up completion semantics and story shape do not agree** (PRD §3 “Remaining,” §4.2 FR-8, §4.3 FR-11/FR-12, §11 SM-2; architecture AD-22; epics Story 3.3/3.4) — Explicit Complete may preserve `Estimated − Done > 0`, yet a Completed item contributes 0; FR-11/SM-2 still formulate totals in terms of “own Remaining.” The current epic also specifies recursive direct-child/per-child-child sequence rather than flattened per-descendant LWW, and omits Unit inheritance, unestimated-descendant count, unavailable state, and the Registry freshness witness. *Fix:* Name the retained meter value and effective Roll-Up contribution separately, define terminal contribution as 0, and rewrite Stories 3.3–3.4 around per-descendant sequence slots, registry-watermark acknowledgement, Unit inheritance, mixed-Unit subtotals, unestimated count, unavailable/repair behavior, and SM-2 quiescence.
- **[high] Rebuild safety and the tenant control-plane exception are still design placeholders** (PRD §4.3 FR-11, §9 Tenant isolation, §13 residual VAL-H08/VAL-H09; architecture AD-16, AD-22, open register; epics NFR-4 and Story 4.9) — The product contract promises reader-safe unavailable/rebuild behavior and universal tenant scoping while also acknowledging a possible global recovery-registry exception. The capture-through-Commit fence and governed namespace/ownership exception remain open, so platform and security teams must guess key namespaces, authority, fencing epochs, reader behavior, and audit evidence. *Fix:* Publish the namespace/owner/authorizer table and the R4 fence state machine (capture, staged writes, promotion, catch-up, rollback), define the only permitted control-plane exception and its audit/retention rules, and add cross-tenant mutation tests plus reader/writer race tests to Story 4.9.
- **[high] Serialized-contract rollout is blocked by an unbound compatibility matrix** (PRD §4.2 FR-7 and §8; addendum Domain Event catalog and Registry protocol; architecture VAL-H11; epics Story 1.5 / NFR-12) — v1 adds Registry commands/events, a dedicated spawn rejection, and read-model fields under an “additive-tolerant” rule, but no accepted reader/writer compatibility matrix defines unknown enum values, unknown polymorphic types, required/default fields, rollout direction, or downgrade behavior. A golden corpus alone cannot choose those semantics. *Fix:* Before any catalog change ships, bind N↔N+1 reader/writer cases, unknown enum/type policy, defaulting and field-removal prohibition, producer-first/consumer-first rollout order, downgrade stance, and golden-corpus tests for every serialized contract.
- **[high] Immutable Raw-Act privacy is a production phase blocker with no owner or acceptance path** (PRD §4.2 FR-7, §9 Observability, §10 Audit; architecture VAL-H12; addendum Raw-Act sketches) — Raw Acts intentionally retain actor, timestamp, verbatim values, and optional notes, while the event log is immutable; the PRD does not define data classification, lawful retention, erasure/anonymization, subject access, or how linked Conversation content and bounded notes differ. VAL-H12 correctly blocks production data, but no story owns it. *Fix:* Add a privacy/security story owned across Product, Platform, and module teams that classifies every event field, pins retention and access, defines erasure/tombstone or cryptographic-erasure behavior compatible with replay, and proves logs/projections/rebuilds honor the policy before production data admission.

### Medium

- **[medium] Performance acceptance is simultaneously numeric and provisional** (PRD §9 Performance, §11 SM-2, §14 Assumptions Index; epics NFR-7) — The PRD says no numeric budget is pinned, then supplies `< 200 ms` reads and `5 s` convergence for a ~1,600-item fixture; epics still say no numeric budgets. Test and platform teams cannot know whether failure blocks release. *Fix:* Either promote the fixture and thresholds to v1 acceptance with environment/percentile/load definitions, or label them non-gating benchmark observations and assign an owner/date for binding real budgets.
- **[medium] Cross-repository story sequencing is described but not executable** (PRD §6.1 and §8; architecture AD-20 R4/R6/R7/R11; epics Epic 3 predecessor and Story 4.9) — Works owns domain intents/translators, EventStore owns generic seams, and Platform owns composition/policy, but there is no dependency graph with versioned interface deliverables, provider/consumer contract tests, or merge/release order. “Add it to the platform first” is direction, not a runnable plan. *Fix:* Create a dependency ledger for each R-row with provider repository, interface artifact/version, owner, consumer story, contract-test location, acceptance evidence, and merge/deployment order; block dependent Works stories until the provider seam is available.
- **[medium] Channel-change acceptance can authorize an impossible active-state test** (PRD §4.2 FR-6, §4.5 FR-17, §11 SM-5; epics Story 4.2) — Active `InProgress`/`Suspended` work rejects Assign, but SM-5 says Channel changes “mid-work” through a state-preserving reassign; Story 4.2 does not constrain the source state. *Fix:* State that SM-5 executes from `Assigned`, or add a distinct active-state binding-change product decision; update Story 4.2 with accepted/rejected source-state cases.

### Low

- **[low] Mechanical source links and FR ordering will mislead automation** (PRD §0; addendum introduction; PRD §4.4 FR-26) — The brief/brainstorm relative links are off by one directory from this workspace, and FR-26 is inserted between FR-16 and FR-17 while downstream artifacts still use range shorthand. *Fix:* Correct the relative paths and add a machine-readable FR inventory/coverage table that lists FR-26 explicitly instead of relying only on numeric ranges.

## Required unblocking sequence

1. Freeze product semantics: Resume duplicate/non-match, ordinary Create versus registry-backed child Create, and completed-item Roll-Up contribution.
2. Bind architecture prerequisites: VAL-H10 first, then executable R4/R6/R7 contracts; close VAL-H09/H11 before affected rollout and VAL-H12 before production data.
3. Correct-course architecture and epics to the 2026-09-08 PRD, including FR-26 and §9 NFR coverage.
4. Reslice Registry/Reactor/platform work along provider-consumer boundaries and add deterministic acknowledgement, recovery, security, compatibility, and privacy tests.
5. Re-run implementation-readiness only after every critical/high finding has an owning story and no dependent story must infer behavior from stale prose.
