Hexalith.Works — Validation Report

_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-works-2026-06-14/prd.md (+ addendum.md)
Fair

This is a genuinely good capability-spec PRD: the thesis is sharp and product-specific ("everything is a Party"; raw act canonical, interpretation a projection), scope is tightened honestly against its own brief, every inferred decision is tagged and indexed, and the two September amendments (FR-20 tie-break, FR-21 LinkConversation) are exemplary in precision. What is at risk is Done-ness at the seams between FRs: the lifecycle has no transition or event for an Assigned item starting work, FR-15 and §10 disagree on whether a non-matching resume is a rejection or a no-op, the actor of non-executor acts (assign, cancel, re-estimate) is not captured despite being the second bet, and an undefined "type" concept backs two configuration knobs. With Epics 1–4 built, these gaps have almost certainly been resolved in code without the PRD recording the answer — the practical risk is the PRD ceasing to be the system of record for the contract it claims to own.

The two extra reviewers sharpen that risk into a verdict. The adversarial pass argues that the purity rules (a clock-free, single-aggregate Handle) are incompatible with three behaviours the PRD also mandates — spawn-with-parent-link, child-completion resume, cascade-cancel — and that the single Executor Binding cannot hold the approver/observer roles the roadmap depends on; it rates both critical. The downstream-drift pass confirms the first of those was in fact answered on 2026-09-06 by the architecture (AD-21 Work-Tree Registry, a second aggregate that takes edge ownership and the public spawn entry away from WorkItem) with no PRD amendment and no decision-log entry, and that all six §13 Open Questions are already resolved by AD decisions the PRD does not cite. Grade is Fair on the rubric (all dimensions strong/adequate, two high findings). If the two adversarial criticals are accepted as written during triage, the grading rule yields Poor; if the AD-21 correct-course pass is run, the second critical collapses into the drift finding. Overall drift verdict: material drift — not superseded, but the PRD lags the architecture on one structural decision and its audit trail stops in June.

Decision-readiness

adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions with dates and reasons, not as considerations: the §0 "Scope decision (confirmed 2026-06-14)", the §1 "defining bet", the §5 "kernel-only surface decision" that explicitly defers MCP/CLI against the brainstorm's Theme 2 filing, FR-10's "cascade-cancel chosen over orphaning", FR-13's "The brainstorm states no limit — proposing a guard", and §6.2's "needs no v1 event placeholder" with reasoning. §2.2 names "the one place the PRD deliberately tightens the brief's 'day one' language". §13 cleanly separates the eight draft OQs resolved by acceptance from six architecture-phase mechanism questions, and the counter-metrics (SM-C1, SM-C2) are real trade-offs.

Two things keep this from strong. First, there is not a single [NOTE FOR PM] in the document, and there is at least one real tension that deserved one: v1 carries AuthorityLevel but enforces nothing (FR-19), Cancel is "carried-not-enforced in v1" (FR-10), any tenant Executor may claim (FR-18 assumption), and Cancel/Expire cascade to the whole open subtree (FR-10). Read together, any Party in a tenant can kill any Work Tree in v1; FR-9's "An authorized Executor" quietly implies a gate that does not exist. Second, §13 OQ6 ("Validation domains") files product semantics — may ProgressReported carry a negative delta, is Unit immutable after first use — under "mechanism decisions, not product-requirement gaps". Those are domain rules a story writer needs, not ETag strategy.

Strategic coherence

strong

The PRD has a thesis and the features serve it. Bet 1 (uniform Executor Binding) is realized by §4.5 and validated by SM-3; the "one number" promise by §4.3 and SM-2; the durable-saga promise by §4.4 and SM-1; thin-core purity by §4.6 and SM-4. Prioritization follows from the thesis — v1 is the foundation slice that makes Themes 3–6 additive, and §6.2 names the seam each theme builds on. The scope kind is coherently platform: SMs are build signals, not usage metrics, and the PRD says why. Counter-metrics exist and bite.

The one gap: §1 declares a second bet — "the canonical event is the raw act, and any interpretation of it is a recomputable projection" — and no SM validates it. FR-7's raw-act rule and §9's "Projections are rebuildable" are both unmeasured, as are the riskiest v1 behaviours (FR-18 race, FR-10 cascade, FR-20 ordering). For a build-signal acceptance model, an untested bet is a bet the build cannot fail.

Done-ness clarity

adequate

Structurally this is the right shape: all 25 FRs carry "Consequences (testable)", the event catalog is closed and named, and FR-20 and FR-21 post-amendment are models of what a testable consequence looks like. FR-11's idempotency-under-replay, FR-13's cross-tenant rejection, FR-22's "compiles … without any IExecutorRouter implementation wired", and FR-24's "contains no *.AppHost" are all mechanically checkable.

The weakness is between FRs. FR-6 gives a forward path Created → Assigned | Queued → InProgress and FR-7 gives a closed event catalog, but nothing moves an Assigned item to InProgress. FR-15 says a resume "whose key matches no current Await-Condition is a domain rejection" while §10 says the same case "is a no-op"; and the aggregate cannot tell "duplicate of an already-applied resume" from "never matched" unless it retains consumed Await-Conditions, which no FR requires. FR-7 promises "who acted" but sources the acting Party from "the binding and the EventStore envelope" — the assigner, canceller, or re-estimator is generally not the bound Executor, and no command in the PRD carries an actor. These are not edge cases; they are the start of the lifecycle, the idempotency contract, and the second bet.

Adversarial review

review-adversarial-general.md

The PRD's two bets — "everything is a Party" and "AI in the loop but never in the system-of-record" — are both declared proven by v1 while v1 ships nothing that could disprove them: no adapter inspects the Channel, no policy reads the AuthorityLevel, no interpretation is ever projected, and every Success Metric reduces to "the build is green under Aspire." Underneath that, the document's purity rules (a clock-free, single-aggregate Handle) are incompatible with three behaviours it also mandates (spawn-with-parent-link, child-completion resume, cascade-cancel), and it names an undefined "engine" to paper over the gap. With implementation several epics in, every one of these ambiguities has already been resolved somewhere by an engineer — the FR-20 correct-course shows the pattern — and the PRD as written cannot tell you whether that resolution is compliant.

critical

The keystone binding cannot hold the roles the roadmap needs, and SM-3 cannot fail in v1

§4.5 FR-17/FR-19, §11 SM-3, §12
FR-17 gives a Work Item "its single Executor Binding"; FR-19's proposed set includes Read = await/observe and Administer = approve spend, set caps, cancel. A single slot cannot carry a Contributor doing the work and an Administer approving its spend, an observer, or an escalation ladder's next rung — exactly the Theme 4/6 shapes §12 claims this seam enables. Meanwhile "no domain code branches on executor kind" is trivially true of a kernel that stores three fields and never reads them; the branching moves into adapters where SM-3 does not look.
Fix: Either make the binding a set (one primary Executor plus zero-or-more role bindings) now, or state explicitly that approvers/observers are not bindings and name where Theme 6 will put them. Rewrite SM-3 to something that can fail: e.g. "adding a fourth Channel value requires zero changes in Server/Projections."
critical

Three cross-aggregate behaviours are required but have no owner under the purity rules

§4.4 FR-15/FR-16, §4.2 FR-10 cascade, §4.3 FR-13, §9 Domain purity, §4.7 FR-24
Handle "never reads a clock or an outside system," yet FR-16 creates a child and emits on the parent (one command, two aggregates); FR-15 says a child's WorkItemCompleted raises a resume command to a parent; FR-10 cascades cancel to descendants; FR-13 rejects cycles and cross-tenant links, which requires reading the other aggregate. UJ-3 attributes this to "the engine," the addendum to "internally," and FR-24 forbids Works from owning "subscription plumbing." Two teams will build this differently and both claim compliance. Synthesis note: the drift review shows the architecture answered this on 2026-09-06 with AD-21 (Work-Tree Registry aggregate + reactor-only SpawnChild) without amending the PRD.
Fix: Add an FR that names the process-manager/saga component, which package owns it, and its consistency guarantees (which aggregate is written first on spawn; whether cascade is synchronous or eventual; what a child does with ProgressReported after its parent is terminal but before the cascade reaches it). In practice: run the AD-21 correct-course into the PRD.
high

"Done = Remaining is 0" is three completion paths, and the auto path is irreversible

§3 Burn-Down/Remaining, §4.2 FR-8/FR-9
FR-8 auto-completes the moment Remaining hits 0; FR-8 also says an unestimated item "completes only by an explicit complete act"; FR-9 says ReEstimated "adjusts Estimated (and therefore Remaining)," so a downward re-estimate below Done silently completes the item. FR-9 also says "over-run and partial progress are native, not errors" — but an executor who reports the full estimate before actually finishing gets auto-completed into a terminal state and can never re-estimate. Every client will implement "Complete" as a synthetic re-estimate-to-done — some emitting three events, some one.
Fix: Decide whether Remaining=0 is a precondition for an explicit Complete (recommended: one completion act, one event) or an auto-trigger; if auto, add a Reopen transition or drop "over-run is native"; forbid ReEstimated below Done, or specify that it completes.
high

Roll-Up requirements are mutually underdetermined; the "single number" promise is withdrawn by FR-12

§1 Vision, §4.3 FR-11/FR-12, §11 SM-2
Vision: "an objective's all-in remaining work is a single number." FR-12: mixed-Unit subtrees expose "per-Unit subtotals rather than a coerced single figure" — and nothing prevents a child spawned in tokens under a parent in hours. FR-11 demands both "reflected incrementally" and "idempotent under at-least-once, possibly out-of-order event delivery"; delta-accumulation satisfies the first and fails the second. SM-2 is unfalsifiable against an eventually-consistent projection with no convergence bound: any failing read is "stale but converging."
Fix: Require the projection to store per-child last-known Remaining (state-based, not delta-based) and name the quiescence condition SM-2 is asserted under. Either constrain Unit inheritance at spawn or downgrade the Vision claim.
high

Expire smuggles a scheduler, an undefined "type" concept, and actor-less raw acts into a kernel that ships no adapters

§4.2 FR-10, §4.4 FR-15, §4.7, §13 OQ-4/OQ-6, §10 Audit
Who fires Expire? FR-15 says timers arrive from "a timer/scheduler adapter" which OQ-4 defers and §4.7 says does not ship, yet SM-1 requires date-triggered resume end-to-end. "Per-type TTL" and FR-13's "per tenant/type" introduce a type that appears nowhere. §10 defines a Raw Act as "acting Party + timestamp + verbatim payload" — a timer-fired WorkItemExpired, or a cascaded WorkItemCancelled on a child, has no acting Party; the non-repudiation model has a hole for every system-originated event. A parent Suspended on a child expires by its own Due Date and cascades onto a child that is 90 % done and not overdue.
Fix: Add the timer/scheduler adapter to v1 scope explicitly and name its owner; define "type" or delete the two references; specify how configuration enters Handle; define the actor for system-originated events; state whether Expire is legal from Suspended.
high

v1's own scope statement contradicts itself and strands work

§0, §5, §12
§0: "v1 (the foundation: Themes 1 & 2)." §5: MCP/CLI were filed "under foundation Theme 2; v1 deliberately defers even these." So v1 ≠ Themes 1 & 2, and the deferred Theme-2 items appear in no §12 roadmap row. They are homeless, which is how deferred work becomes forgotten work.
Fix: Restate scope as "Theme 1 + the kernel subset of Theme 2" and add a roadmap row (or a Theme 3 sub-row) for the non-LLM command surfaces.
medium

The "what's next" query has no defined consumer; the September tiebreak is arbitrary and called a decision

§4.6 FR-20, §4.1 FR-4, §13 OQ-2
FR-20 returns "a tenant's claimable/assigned Work Items" — one list mixing everyone's Assigned items with the shared Queued pool. For whom is that "next"? The amendment fixes the tiebreak to WorkItemId ordinal while conceding creation-order approximation is "guidance, not an enforced contract". "applies query-side authorization/result filtering" names no criterion. Priority's type and direction (OQ-2) are deferred, so FR-4's "sorts last" is untestable.
Fix: Split into two queries (per-executor and per-tenant pool) or add an executor parameter; name the filter predicate; commit to Priority's ordering direction now; if the id-ordinal tiebreak is acceptable, say so as a product decision.
medium

The thin core leaks at four visible seams

§4.2 FR-7, §4.6 FR-21/FR-22, §3 Obligation, §11 SM-C2
FR-7: events "may carry an optional human-readable note"; FR-21: "Works holds the correlation ID rather than its own comment store." A note-per-event is a comment store. Obligation's "human-readable description" is unbounded, so a 10 KB markdown brief in the aggregate is compliant. FR-22 mandates IExecutorRouter with no implementation and no caller, and IExpectationResolver whose only implementation returns a default — dead abstractions that SM-C2 calls "a negative" when anyone else does it.
Fix: Bound the description length and forbid notes on events (or admit Works owns a minimal note stream and reconcile FR-21); drop IExecutorRouter from v1 and defer IExpectationResolver until it has a non-trivial caller.
low

Why Now argues for the synthesis and v1 ships none of it

§7
"the whitespace is the synthesis — one audited object where the backlog, the durable saga, and the effort+cost ledger are the same thing." v1 has no cost ledger, no interpretation, no routing, and no channel; the timing argument justifies Themes 3–6, not a headless kernel.
Fix: Either state the v1-specific timing pressure (e.g. a named sibling module blocked on Works) or move §7 to the brief and replace it with one sentence.

Downstream drift review

review-downstream-drift.md

It is no longer the source of record for the kernel's shape: the 2026-09-06 architecture update introduced a second aggregate (AD-21 Work-Tree Registry) and declared its own register the binding contract — none of which was routed back through the PRD or the decision log. Verdict: material drift. Checks with no finding: all approved edits present (three wording deltas noted); all 25 FRs mapped to an epic and at least one story — no orphan FRs.
