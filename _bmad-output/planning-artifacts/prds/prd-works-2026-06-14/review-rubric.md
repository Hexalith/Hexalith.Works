# PRD Quality Review — Hexalith.Works

*Reviewed 2026-09-08 against `prd.md` (updated 2026-09-05, FR-20 amended 2026-09-06) and `addendum.md`. Calibration: chain-top, launch-stakes technical capability spec feeding architecture, epics/stories, and an active implementation (Epics 1–4 largely built).*

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely good capability-spec PRD: the thesis is sharp and product-specific ("everything is a Party"; raw act canonical, interpretation a projection), scope is tightened honestly against its own brief, every inferred decision is tagged and indexed, and the two September amendments (FR-20 tie-break, FR-21 `LinkConversation`) are exemplary in precision. What is at risk is Done-ness at the seams between FRs: the lifecycle has no transition or event for an `Assigned` item starting work, FR-15 and §10 disagree on whether a non-matching resume is a rejection or a no-op, the actor of non-executor acts (assign, cancel, re-estimate) is not captured despite being the second bet, and an undefined "type" concept backs two configuration knobs. With Epics 1–4 built, these gaps have almost certainly been resolved in code without the PRD recording the answer — the practical risk is the PRD ceasing to be the system of record for the contract it claims to own.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions with dates and reasons, not as considerations: the §0 "Scope decision (confirmed 2026-06-14)", the §1 "defining bet", the §5 "kernel-only surface decision" that explicitly defers MCP/CLI "even these non-LLM command surfaces" against the brainstorm's Theme 2 filing, FR-10's "cascade-cancel chosen over orphaning", FR-13's "The brainstorm states no limit — proposing a guard", and §6.2's "needs **no v1 event placeholder**" with reasoning. §2.2 names "the one place the PRD deliberately tightens the brief's 'day one' language" — a rare and welcome act of naming a divergence from an upstream artifact. §13 cleanly separates the eight draft OQs resolved by acceptance from six architecture-phase mechanism questions, and the counter-metrics (SM-C1, SM-C2) are real trade-offs, not decoration.

Two things keep this from strong. First, there is not a single `[NOTE FOR PM]` in the document, and there is at least one real tension that deserved one: v1 carries AuthorityLevel but enforces nothing (FR-19), Cancel is "carried-not-enforced in v1" (FR-10), any tenant Executor may claim (FR-18 assumption), and Cancel/Expire cascade to the whole open subtree (FR-10). Read together, any Party in a tenant can kill any Work Tree in v1. The PRD never says this in one place; FR-9's "An authorized Executor" quietly implies a gate that does not exist. Second, §13 OQ6 ("Validation domains") files product semantics — may `ProgressReported` carry a negative delta, is Unit immutable after first use — under "mechanism decisions, not product-requirement gaps". Those are domain rules a story writer needs, not ETag strategy.

### Findings
- **[medium]** v1 authority blast radius unsurfaced (FR-9, FR-10 Cancel/Cascade, FR-18 assumption, FR-19) — "authorized Executor" (FR-9) has no v1 meaning; combined with cascade-cancel and open claiming, any tenant Party can terminate any subtree. No `[NOTE FOR PM]` marks this. *Fix:* add an explicit v1 statement (in §10 or FR-19) that all acts are ungated in v1 and cascade is unguarded, with a `[NOTE FOR PM]` recording that this is accepted for a headless kernel exercised only by builders; replace "authorized Executor" in FR-9 with "any bound Executor (authority carried-not-enforced)".
- **[medium]** Product semantics filed as architecture mechanism (§13 OQ6) — negative progress deltas, Unit immutability after first use, and the TTL configuration source govern what an Executor may do, not how it is stored. *Fix:* decide the first two in FR-8/FR-3 consequences (e.g., "a negative Done delta is a domain rejection; use `ReEstimated` to undo"; "Unit is fixed at first estimate; changing it is a rejection") and leave only the configuration source in §13.
- **[low]** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts anywhere (whole document) — every tension is either resolved-by-acceptance (§14) or pushed to "theme time" (§12). That is defensible for a foundation slice, but the §12 "Designed-for tensions" (cost-aware scheduling reaching into kernel-owned Schedule) already shape v1 seams and should be PM-visible now. *Fix:* promote the §12 tensions paragraph to `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts with an owner.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here is furniture. The Vision (§1) could not be dropped into another PRD — "burns down toward zero remaining effort", "late-resolved reference to a sibling Hexalith module, never copied", "AI in the loop but never in the system-of-record" are load-bearing and reappear as FR consequences. §2.1 uses JTBD rather than personas, and the builder job drives the v1 cut visibly. NFRs (§9) are substrate-specific — `{tenant}:{domain}:{aggregateId}`, persist-then-publish, `IRejectionEvent`, query-side authorization "in addition to command-side checks" — not "must be secure and scalable"; the one qualitative NFR (Performance) says so and tags itself. §7 Why Now is short and self-deprecating ("no moat on any single primitive; the advantage is coherence"), which is more honest than most differentiation sections.

### Findings
- **[low]** SM-5 duplicates SM-3 (§11) — "Handoff = one operation" is the third consequence of FR-17 ("Human→AI and AI→human handoff is the same reassign operation"), which SM-3 already validates. *Fix:* fold SM-5 into SM-3, or repoint it at the Glossary's uncovered claim that "a Party may change Channel mid-work" (see Done-ness).

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD has a thesis and the features serve it. Bet 1 (uniform Executor Binding) is realized by §4.5 and validated by SM-3; the "one number" promise by §4.3 and SM-2; the durable-saga promise by §4.4 and SM-1; thin-core purity by §4.6 and SM-4. Prioritization follows from the thesis — v1 is the foundation slice that makes Themes 3–6 additive, and §6.2 names the seam each theme builds on. The scope kind is coherently *platform*: SMs are build signals, not usage metrics, and the PRD says why. Counter-metrics exist and bite ("capability … migrating *out* of Works is success, not regression").

The one gap: §1 declares a *second* bet — "the canonical event is the raw act, and any interpretation of it is a recomputable projection" — and no SM validates it. FR-7's "Events store the Raw Act … not interpreted/derived values" and §9's "Projections are rebuildable" are both unmeasured. Several of the riskiest v1 behaviours (FR-18 single-claim-wins race, FR-10 cascade, FR-20 ordering) likewise have no SM. For a build-signal acceptance model, an untested bet is a bet the build cannot fail.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Bet 2 and rebuildability have no success metric (§1, FR-7, §9 "Projections are rebuildable", §11) — SM-1–SM-5 validate FR-1, 6–8, 11–17, 19, 21–25 only; FR-7's raw-act rule, §9 rebuildability, FR-10 cascade, FR-18 race, and FR-20 ordering are unvalidated. *Fix:* add SM-6 "Rebuild from zero: dropping and replaying Roll-Up and 'what's next' projections reproduces identical read models; no event payload carries a derived value" (validates FR-7, FR-11, FR-20, §9), and extend SM-1 or add a secondary SM covering FR-18 race and FR-10 cascade.
- **[low]** "Themes 1 & 2" never defined in the PRD (§0, §6.1 heading, §5) — the v1 scope unit is imported from the brief; §12 defines Themes 3–6 but a reader of this document alone cannot say what Theme 1 or Theme 2 is. *Fix:* one clause in §0 or §6.1 naming them (e.g., "Theme 1 — Work Item kernel; Theme 2 — executor binding & thin-core boundaries").

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Structurally this is the right shape: all 25 FRs carry "Consequences (testable)", the event catalog is closed and named, and FR-20 and FR-21 post-amendment are models of what a testable consequence looks like ("`WorkItemId` ordinal, a replay-stable strict total order"; "The first link is authoritative … a different ID is a domain rejection that leaves state unchanged"). FR-11's idempotency-under-replay, FR-13's cross-tenant rejection, FR-22's "compiles … without any `IExecutorRouter` implementation wired", and FR-24's "contains no `*.AppHost`" are all mechanically checkable.

The weakness is between FRs. FR-6 gives a forward path `Created → Assigned | Queued → InProgress` and FR-7 gives a closed event catalog, but nothing moves an `Assigned` item to `InProgress`: `WorkItemClaimed` covers the pull entry only; there is no `WorkItemStarted`, and the PRD does not say that the first `ProgressReported` is the implicit start. FR-15 says a resume "whose key matches no current Await-Condition is a domain rejection" while §10 says the same case "is a no-op"; and the aggregate cannot tell "duplicate of an already-applied resume" from "never matched" unless it retains consumed Await-Conditions, which no FR requires. FR-7 promises "who acted" but sources the acting Party from "the binding and the EventStore envelope" — the assigner, canceller, or re-estimator is generally *not* the bound Executor, and no command in the PRD carries an actor. These are not edge cases; they are the start of the lifecycle, the idempotency contract, and the second bet.

### Findings
- **[high]** No transition or event for `Assigned → InProgress` (FR-6, FR-7, FR-18) — the catalog has `WorkItemClaimed` for `Queued → InProgress` but nothing for a pushed item starting; `Created → InProgress` and whether `ProgressReported` is legal in `Created`/`Assigned`/`Queued` are also unstated. *Fix:* publish the full legal-transition table in FR-6 (from-state × command → to-state/event) and either add `WorkItemStarted` to FR-7 or state "the first `ProgressReported` on an `Assigned` item transitions it to `InProgress`".
- **[high]** FR-15 and §10 contradict on non-matching resume — FR-15: "matches no current Await-Condition is a domain rejection"; §10: "no longer matches an Await-Condition is a no-op — FR-15". The "duplicate … idempotent no-op" branch is undecidable without retained history. *Fix:* pick one rule; if the no-op branch is kept, add a consequence that consumed/superseded Await-Conditions are retained on the aggregate (or that any resume on a non-`Suspended` item is a no-op), and align §10 wording.
- **[medium]** Acting Party not captured for non-executor acts (FR-7, FR-9, FR-10, FR-17) — "capturing the acting Party … via the binding and the EventStore envelope" fails for Assign, Reassign, Cancel, ReEstimate, Reschedule, where the actor is not the bound Executor; "Works does not populate envelope metadata". Bet 2 ("who acted, when, and the verbatim payload") is not met. *Fix:* require every command to carry `ActorPartyId` and every Raw-Act event to record it in payload; add a negative test that an event without an actor is rejected.
- **[medium]** Unestimated item's Roll-Up contribution undefined (FR-1 "undefined-until-estimated", FR-11, FR-12) — an item with no Estimated has no Remaining and no Unit; whether it contributes 0, is excluded, or is surfaced as "unestimated count" in per-Unit subtotals is unstated. *Fix:* add to FR-11: "an unestimated item contributes 0 to rolled Remaining and increments an `UnestimatedDescendants` count exposed alongside the subtotals".
- **[medium]** Creation with an Executor Binding yields Status `Created`, but `Assigned` is defined as "a specific Executor bound" (FR-1 consequence 2, FR-6 consequence 2) — an engineer cannot tell whether `WorkItemCreated` with a binding lands in `Created` (requiring a redundant Assign) or `Assigned`. *Fix:* state in FR-1: "if an Executor Binding is supplied, creation emits `WorkItemCreated` followed by `WorkItemAssigned` and Status is `Assigned`" (or the alternative).
- **[medium]** Expire has no owner, an undefined knob, and a precedence conflict (FR-10 Expire, FR-15, §13 OQ4) — "fires when the Due Date passes" is passive; FR-15 names a timer adapter for *resume* only and OQ4 covers resume only; "a configured per-type TTL" references a Work Item "type" that does not exist in §3; and a `Suspended` item parked on a date with a passed Due Date is both "resumes" (FR-5) and "expires" (FR-10). *Fix:* add an `ExpireWorkItem` command raised by the same timer/scheduler adapter, define precedence (e.g., "Expire wins over DateReached resume when both are due"), and either define "type" in §3 or replace with "per tenant".
- **[medium]** Reject semantics under-specified (FR-6, FR-10 Reject, FR-18) — `Rejected` is listed as a terminal Status, yet the default `WorkItemRejected` outcome is Status `Queued`; whether requeue-by-reject also emits `WorkItemQueued` (FR-18 says it "marks every entry into the queue") and from which states Reject is legal (`Assigned` only, or `InProgress` too) are unstated. *Fix:* specify legal from-states, and state "requeue-by-reject emits `WorkItemRejected` then `WorkItemQueued`" or "emits `WorkItemRejected` only, whose default effect is Status `Queued`".
- **[medium]** Performance NFR is an adjective (§9 Performance) — "remain responsive for realistically deep/wide trees" is unfalsifiable; the assumption tag is honest but SM-2 still needs a fixture size to be a build signal. *Fix:* pin a v1 test-tree shape (e.g., depth 32 × fan-out 50, 10⁴ nodes) and a convergence bound for the Roll-Up after a leaf event (e.g., "< N seconds under the platform-owned Aspire topology"), tagged `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **[low]** Channel change mid-work has no FR (§3 Channel: "a Party may change Channel mid-work", FR-17) — unclear whether it is a reassign (emits `WorkItemAssigned`) or a distinct act. *Fix:* add a FR-17 consequence: "changing Channel on the same Party is a reassign that emits `WorkItemAssigned` with the new binding".
- **[low]** Fate of remaining Await-Conditions after first match unstated (FR-5, FR-15) — after a date resumes the item, does the child-completion trigger later cause a rejection, a no-op, or a second resume? *Fix:* "resume clears all Await-Conditions; later triggers for cleared conditions follow the FR-15 non-matching rule".
- **[low]** `ChildSpawned` vs the child's own `WorkItemCreated` (FR-16) — "creates a child Work Item (FR-1 semantics) … and emits on the parent" does not say whether the child stream also receives `WorkItemCreated`, nor whether spawn-and-suspend is one command or two. *Fix:* state both streams' events and the command shape.
- **[low]** `ReEstimated` below Done (FR-8, FR-9) — Estimated set below current Done clamps Remaining to 0; whether that auto-emits `WorkItemCompleted` is unstated. *Fix:* one consequence in FR-9.
- **[low]** "Reference to an Expectation" is unlocated (FR-1, FR-2, addendum port sketch) — FR-1/FR-2 say the Obligation holds an "optional Expectation reference", the FR-2 assumption says it is "referenced/resolved on demand, not stored", and the addendum's `Resolve(WorkItemState state)` takes no reference. An engineer cannot tell whether a field exists on the aggregate. *Fix:* either name the stored field (an `ExpectationId`?) or drop "reference" and say "resolved on demand from state".

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 Non-Goals does real work: it names four things Works is *not*, and the parenthetical about MCP/CLI ("The brainstorm filed … under foundation Theme 2; v1 deliberately defers even these") is exactly the kind of upstream-contradicting de-scope that is usually done silently. §2.2 Non-Users explains *why* the brief's co-primary audiences are not v1 consumers. §6.2 attaches a "Seam laid" note to every deferred theme. All 19 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed in §14 with the confirmation date, and §13's residue is small (six) and genuinely architectural except OQ6 (see Decision-readiness). Open-items density for a green-light PRD is fine because the assumptions are confirmed, not pending.

The one scope ambiguity: §6.1 lists "date/timer native" as in scope and SM-1 requires the full saga under the Aspire topology, but the timer/scheduler adapter that makes date resume (and Expire) happen is neither a §6.1 deliverable nor a §6.2 deferral — §13 OQ4 defers its identity. §6.2's "Any production channel adapter" does not obviously cover a scheduler.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Timer/scheduler adapter is load-bearing but unowned (§6.1 "date/timer native", FR-15 Date/timer, FR-10 Expire, §13 OQ4) — date-driven resume and expiry cannot be exercised in SM-1 without it, yet it is in neither In Scope nor Out of Scope. *Fix:* add to §6.1 "a minimal timer/scheduler adapter (test-harness grade) that raises resume/expire commands", or move date/timer resume to §6.2 and drop it from SM-1.

## Downstream usability — strong

For a chain-top PRD this is where the discipline shows: FR-1–FR-25 contiguous and unique, UJ-1–UJ-4, SM-1–SM-5 + SM-C1–C2; every §/FR/SM cross-reference in the body resolves (checked FR-3→12, FR-4→9/20, FR-5→13, FR-6→10/15/18, FR-7→9/21, FR-10→11/19, FR-13→11, FR-16→1/13, FR-17→SM-3, FR-18/20→§9, §9→FR-11/15/18/20/22, §10→FR-11/15, §11→FRs, §13→FRs). Each feature names the UJ it realizes and each SM the FRs it validates, so epics can be cut by feature and stories by FR consequence. The addendum is correctly non-binding and consistent with the FRs, including the amended `LinkConversation` semantics.

Two things a story writer will trip on. "type" appears as a configuration dimension in FR-10 ("per-type TTL"), FR-13 ("configurable per tenant/type"), and §12 ("per-type data policy") but there is no Work Item type in the Glossary, the aggregate description (§4.1), or FR-1 — a story for either knob has to invent a taxonomy. And amendment provenance is uneven: FR-20 carries "*(Amended 2026-09-06 …)*", but the frontmatter's `updated: 2026-09-05` has no corresponding marker anywhere; a downstream reader cannot tell which of FR-15, FR-21, or §4.7 changed from the June baseline that the epics were cut from.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Undefined "type" backs two configuration knobs (FR-10 Expire, FR-13 depth, §12 Theme 4) — no Work Item type exists in §3 or §4.1. *Fix:* either add `Type` (or `Kind`) to the Glossary and FR-1 as an optional discriminator, or change both knobs to "per tenant" and leave per-type policy to Theme 4.
- **[medium]** 2026-09-05 amendments unmarked (frontmatter `updated: 2026-09-05`; only FR-20 carries an "Amended" note) — with Epics 1–4 built against an earlier text, downstream cannot diff intent from implementation. *Fix:* add a short "Amendment log" after §14 listing date, section, and the correct-course ID for every change since `status: final`, and mark each amended consequence inline as FR-20 does.
- **[low]** "Done" collides (§3 Burn-Down, §3 Remaining, §4.2 description, FR-8) — "Done" is a Burn-Down quantity ("the reported Done delta") *and* the completion state ("**Done = Remaining is 0** is the completion invariant"). *Fix:* rename the invariant to "**Completed ⇔ Remaining = 0**" and reserve "Done" for the quantity.

## Shape fit — strong

The PRD correctly reads itself as a headless technical capability spec inside a brownfield ecosystem and shapes accordingly: UJs are "lean, capability-illustrating narratives" and only four; SMs are build signals with the reason stated; §8 exists because this is a developer product; the substrate constraints are cited by name (`CachingProjectionActor`, `IRejectionEvent`, EventStore domain-service SDK) rather than re-derived; and technical depth is pushed to the addendum so the PRD stays a requirements document. It is neither over-formalized (no persona parade, no UX section for a product with no UI) nor under-formalized (the Glossary and FR consequence discipline are heavier than a standalone spec would need, and that is right for chain-top). The §4.7 amendment toward a platform-owned Aspire AppHost is consistent with the addendum's package-layout rules, which suggests the brownfield references were checked against the real substrate.

### Findings
- **[low]** UJ-3 has no protagonist and UJ-4 sits among v1 journeys (§2.3) — UJ-3's actor is the Work Item itself ("An item spawns a child, parks itself"), and UJ-4 is the only journey with a named person (Mary) but is explicitly not built. Acceptable for a headless kernel, but a story writer scanning §2.3 for the v1 human will find none. *Fix:* give UJ-3 a builder or Party actor issuing the spawn/suspend commands, and move UJ-4 under §12 with a back-reference.
